## What should you look for when choosing an IPTV provider?

Before comparing monthly prices, start with the boring questions. Boring questions are useful because they tend to become expensive questions later.

### 1. Start with the channels you actually watch

A huge channel number looks impressive, but it does not tell you whether the provider has the channels or regional content you care about.

TiviLive currently advertises **28,000+ live channels** and **100,000+ VOD titles**, along with EPG, sports and PPV content. Those numbers are claims published by TiviLive itself, so they are best treated as advertised catalog size rather than an independently verified count.

The smarter test is simple: make a list of your must-have categories before subscribing. Think sports, news, local programming, movies, children's content and international channels. Then use the provider's trial period to check the channels you would actually miss.

That little test is much more useful than admiring a giant number on a homepage.

### 2. Check the devices before checking out

One of the most common IPTV headaches is discovering that the service technically works, just not on the device sitting in front of you.

TiviLive says it supports Smart TVs, Android and iOS devices, Fire TV/Firestick, MAG devices, Kodi, Windows and Mac computers, along with common IPTV players such as IPTV Smarters, TiviMate and VLC. Its installation documentation also provides setup instructions for several of those platforms.

That matters because “supports all devices” and “works nicely on your specific TV” are two different sentences.

A short trial on your actual setup is the cleanest way to find out.

### 3. Understand what “connections” really means

This is one of the most important things to check when learning how to choose an IPTV provider.

A connection generally refers to a simultaneous stream. TiviLive says one connection means one screen streaming at a time, while additional connection options are intended for households with multiple viewers.

So if one person watches the TV while another watches a tablet, a single-connection plan may not fit your household.

The nice part is that TiviLive does not change the advertised core channel and VOD package when you move between connection counts. The main difference is how many simultaneous streams the subscription permits.

## How important is streaming quality?

Very.

An IPTV service can advertise 4K all day long, but your real-world experience depends on the source, device and internet connection.

TiviLive says its service supports 4K, UHD, Full HD, HD and SD depending on the broadcast source. Its FAQ recommends at least 16 Mbps for HD and 25+ Mbps for 4K/UHD, while the terms recommend 10 Mbps for HD and 25 Mbps for 4K.

Because those figures differ slightly between the site's pages, using a stable connection with some headroom is the sensible approach.

A wired Ethernet connection can also be useful for a TV or streaming box. Wi-Fi may work perfectly well, but Wi-Fi has a funny habit of becoming “perfectly well” right up until kickoff.

## One more thing: check content rights

When learning how to choose an IPTV provider, price and channel count are not the only questions.

A legitimate IPTV technology simply delivers television over the internet. What matters for a specific service is whether it has the appropriate rights to distribute the content it offers in your region.

TiviLive's terms say it acts as a reseller rather than a broadcaster and state that streamed content belongs to its respective rights holders.

Before subscribing, check the provider's terms and make sure the channels, sports and on-demand content you intend to watch are legitimately available to you where you live.

## So, how should you choose an IPTV provider?

The easiest approach is to ignore the marketing fireworks for a moment and work through a simple checklist:

**Channel fit:** Does it actually carry what you watch?

**Device support:** Does it work properly on your TV, phone, streaming stick or computer?

**Connections:** Do you need one stream or several at the same time?

**Trial:** Can you test the service on your own setup before paying for a longer term?

**Performance:** Does it remain usable during your normal viewing hours?

**Support:** Can you actually reach someone when something goes wrong?

**Refund terms:** Are the refund rules clear and consistent?

**Content rights:** Can you verify that the provider is authorized to distribute the content you plan to watch?
